# Findings 2026-11-17 — Why S24 looks slower than S23 on Kindi but 2.7× faster on llama.cpp

**Question (owner):** S24 runs llama.cpp ~2.7× faster than S23 (24.4 vs 9.0 tok/s), yet Kindi
verify shows S24 *slower* (0.87 vs 1.33/1.30 tok/s). If the silicon is 2.7× faster, Kindi on S24
"should" be 2–3 tok/s. Is something wrong — possibly a fundamental error in the x86→ARM conversion?

**Verdict: no conversion bug. Three separate causes, all evidenced below:**

1. **The Kindi "inversion" is a thermal-state measurement artifact.** At steady state on the
   identical 158-item workload, S24 runs at **93% of S23's speed** (parity within ~7%). The
   headline verify numbers compared **S23 cold-boosted vs S24 already-warm**.
2. **The 2.7× llama.cpp gap is not a documented silicon result.** The model files are
   byte-identical, the chipset labels in the device-info files are **wrong/swapped**, and the
   measurement conditions (threads, thermal state, screen, build) were never recorded. Kindi
   steady-state parity says the two chips are in the same class — a 2.7× sustained gap is
   implausible and must be re-measured under a controlled protocol.
3. **Kindi ARM's absolute ~1 tok/s is by design, not a bug.** The ARM port is an explicitly
   scalar, correctness-first port of the AVX2 x86 kernel ("NEON SIMD acceleration of rows8()
   is a future step" — source header). Instruction-count math reproduces ~1 tok/s from first
   principles. The engine is bit-exact correct on all three phones; it is simply not yet
   vectorized, so it cannot be compared to llama.cpp's heavily NEON-optimized kernels as an
   engine-vs-engine speed result.

## Evidence A — Steady-state paired comparison: S24 ≈ 93% of S23 (no inversion)

Both phones ran the **same 158 textjobs items in the same order**. Per-item time ratio
(S23 secs / S24 secs = S24 speed relative to S23), paired by job:

| Job block | n | S24/S23 speed ratio (mean) | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sentiment | 30 | 0.884 | 0.755 | 0.955 |
| escalate | 30 | 0.947 | 0.922 | 0.970 |
| spam | 30 | 0.944 | 0.907 | 0.978 |
| topic | 30 | 0.954 | 0.919 | 0.981 |
| spell | 15 | 0.959 | 0.941 | 0.972 |
| grammar | 12 | 0.898 | 0.772 | 0.970 |
| table | 4 | 0.905 | 0.891 | 0.914 |
| **all paired** | **151** | **0.932** | 0.755 | 0.981 |

Under sustained load the devices are within ~5–12% of each other. **There is no 1.5× device
inversion at steady state.** (S24 wall 11,173 s / 158 items; S23 wall 9,244 s / 151 items.)

## Evidence B — Thermal trends inside the run: S23 started cold, S24 started warm

Sentiment block (first 30 items, identical workload), per-item wall seconds:

| Device | first 10 mean | last 10 mean | trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| S23 #2 | **37.6 s** | **42.8 s** | **+14% slower** as it heats up (cold boost → throttle) |
| S24 | **47.2 s** | **45.5 s** | flat/slightly improving (already warm at start) |

By the escalate block (≈35–70 min in): S23 67.9→68.6 s, S24 71.9→72.3 s — both flat, ~6% apart.

The S24 setup note (`2026-11-11-kindi-arm64-android-setup.md`) says the S24 Termux was
"verified during thermal benchmark" — i.e. **S24's verify ran on an already-hot phone**, while
S23's verify (1.30/1.33) was a cold-start number. S23's own warm decode suite confirms it
cannot sustain the cold number: g16–g256 decode = **1.02–1.10 tok/s**, not 1.30.

**The README's "S24 is slower due to thermal throttling" had the attribution backwards:
S23's headline is the cold-boost outlier; S24 was measured pre-throttled.**

## Evidence C — The verify metric is blended, and the two engines' metrics differ

`verify.py` computes `tok/dt` where `tok = len(prompt_ids) + len(generated)` and `dt` is the
wall time of `generate()` **including prefill**. reference.json prompts are 5/5/9/5 tokens:

- S24: 184 tokens / 212 s = **0.87** ✓ (52.0+52.0+56.4+51.6 s)
- S23: 184 tokens / 141.5 s = **1.30** ✓

llama-server's `predicted_per_second` (what `bench.py` reads) is **pure decode**, prefill
excluded. The cross-engine "tok/s" comparison was apples-to-oranges even before thermal state.

## Evidence D — The ARM kernel is scalar by design; ~1 tok/s is what the math predicts

`kcpu_arm.c` header, verbatim:

> "Port of kindi/kcpu.c … from AVX2 to portable C + ARM NEON headers.
> The scalar kernels below are bit-exact with kcpu.c; **NEON SIMD acceleration of rows8()
> is a future step.**"

`arm_neon.h` is included but **no NEON intrinsic is used anywhere**. Compute is scalar FP32;
FP16 appears only as KV-cache *storage* with scalar `h2f`/`f2h` conversions. (The README's
"scalar FP16 implementation" description is wrong.)

The x86 `rows8` inner loop (kcpu.c) is branchless AVX2:
`_mm256_srlv_epi32` (8 nibble extracts / instruction) → `_mm256_permutevar8x32_ps`
(8-lane table gather / instruction) → bit-trick zero-masking → FMA scale.

The ARM `rows8` replaces each vector op with an 8-iteration scalar loop containing shift,
mask, compare, **data-dependent branch** (`if(c0 != 8u)`), dependent table load, add.

Measured from the actual pack (`~/.kindi/pack-2b`, 151 tensors):

- **117.5 M pair-code words per token** → ≈ **11.3 G scalar ops/token** in rows8 alone
- At a phone big-core effective scalar rate of ~8–12 Gops/s → **~0.9–1.4 tok/s** — exactly
  the observed band. The number is not anomalous; it is the predicted speed of this kernel.
- Zero-weight fraction **q = 0.337** → P(code 8) ≈ 11.4% → the `if(code != 8)` branch is
  data-dependent with an ~11% minority class (mispredict penalty the x86 version does not
  pay — it masks branchlessly). ~1.9 G branch decisions/token.
- Effective weight traffic ≈ 0.47 GB codes/token at ~1 tok/s ≈ 0.5 GB/s — **~10–50× below
  phone DRAM bandwidth**: the kernel is instruction/latency-bound, not bandwidth-bound.
  Vectorization, not memory, is the lever.

**Correctness is NOT in question:** 4/4 prompts × 40/40 tokens bit-exact vs `reference.json`
on all three phones, and the smoke oracle matches the desktop x86 oracle exactly. There is
no functional x86→ARM conversion error. The asymmetry is that x86 Kindi's speed comes from
AVX2 vectorization that the ARM port has not implemented yet.

## Evidence E — The llama.cpp 2.7× gap: identical model, wrong chipset labels, no protocol

- `gemma-3-1b-q4km-s23.gguf` and `…-s24.gguf` are **byte-identical**
  (md5 `b76f6e8db2150098da3114da880a25aa`, 806,058,240 B — same file as the A32's).
- **Chipset labels in device-info are wrong/swapped:**
  - S24 `SM-S921B`, codename `e1s` = **Exynos 2400** (the SD 8 Gen 3 S24 is `SM-S921U`/`e1q`;
    the S24 Ultra is `e3q`). Label said "Snapdragon 8 Gen 3".
  - S23 `SM-S911B`, codename `dm1q` = **Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 for Galaxy** (the S23 line was
    Snapdragon-only worldwide; there is no Exynos S23). Label said "Exynos".
  - Verify on-device: `getprop ro.board.platform` / `ro.soc.model`.
- SD 8 Gen 2 and Exynos 2400 are same-class silicon. Kindi steady-state parity (Evidence A)
  independently confirms it. **A 2.7× sustained llama.cpp gap between them is implausible.**
- The vault has **no note recording the 24.4/9.0 measurement conditions** (threads, build
  flags, screen state, thermal state, concurrent load). The numbers exist only in the
  checkpoint summary.
- Corroborating anomaly: **A32 (Dimensity 720, much weaker silicon) also shows ~9–11 tok/s**
  on the same model (vault 2026-11-07: 11.2 standalone / 10.4 served). S23 ≈ A32 while
  S24 = 2.7× both suggests the **S23 run was hampered** (throttled, little-core cpuset
  confinement, fewer threads, or concurrent load — S23 ran 2.56 h of Kindi textjobs on
  11-11), not that SD 8 Gen 2 ≈ Dimensity 720.

## Evidence F — Load-time difference is a storage-path difference

S23 #2 `phone_tests.json` meta: pack = `/data/data/com.termux/files/home/kindi-pack-2b`
(direct app filesystem), load **19.18 s**. S24 setup pushes the pack to `/sdcard/kindi-pack-2b`
(**FUSE**), load **37.7 s**. Load is numpy repack + file reads (OpenMP-independent), so this
is storage/FUSE + single-core, not an engine-threading difference.

## Additional documentation defects found

1. **"4 threads" in the ready log is an env echo, not a measurement.** `cpu.py` prints
   `os.environ.get("OMP_NUM_THREADS", …)`; if the `.so` was built without `-fopenmp` the
   stubs silently run **1 thread** and the log still says 4. The S24 setup recipe's
   `KINDI_CPU_CFLAGS="-O3 -march=armv8.2-a+simd -fno-math-errno"` **omits `-fopenmp`**
   (the default includes it). Whether S24's actual `.so` has OpenMP is unverified —
   check `nm -D libkcpu_arm.so | grep -i omp` on the phone. (Steady-state parity argues
   against a 1-vs-4 thread difference, but it must be ruled out explicitly.)
2. `+simd` is not a valid clang feature flag (`+fp16`/`+fullfp16`/`+dotprod` are); harmless
   here only because the kernel uses no FP16 arithmetic.
3. README "scalar FP16" → actually scalar FP32 compute + FP16 KV storage.
4. README thermal attribution backwards (see Evidence B).
5. device-info chipset labels swapped (see Evidence E).

## Falsifiable next tests (cheap, on-phone, in order)

1. **OpenMP probe (2 min):** `nm -D` the phone's `libkcpu_arm.so` for GOMP/omp symbols;
   during a run, `cat /proc/<pid>/status | grep Threads`. Prediction: 4 worker threads on
   both. If S24 = 1 → rebuild with `-fopenmp`, re-verify; expect S24 to jump toward parity+.
2. **Cold-verify both phones (30 min cooldown, screen on, charger detached, same protocol):**
   record `dumpsys battery` temperature before/after + per-prompt times.
   **Prediction: S24 cold ≈ 1.2–1.4 tok/s (parity with S23 cold).** If S24 cold is still
   ~0.87 → real device/config issue; inspect cpuset (`cat /proc/<pid>/cpuset`) and flags.
3. **Controlled llama.cpp re-run:** same binary + same md5-identical GGUF, `-t 4`, screen on,
   30 min cooldown, 6 requests, log temperature + `predicted_per_second`.
   **Prediction: S23 ≈ 15–22, S24 ≈ 18–26 tok/s; gap shrinks to ~1.2–1.4×.** If S23 stays
   ~9 → investigate cpuset/scheduler/build, not silicon.
4. **Branchless table fix (host code change, then one phone re-run):** extend pair tables to
   9 entries with `t[8] = 0`, delete both `if(code != 8)` branches (matches x86 semantics
   exactly, stays bit-exact). Re-run decode g64 on S23. **Prediction: ≥1.2–1.5× from
   mispredict removal alone.** The big lever remains NEON-vectorizing rows8 (plausible
   5–10×; would put Kindi ARM in the ~6–13 tok/s band on these phones — the first honest
   answer to "is Kindi faster than anything on ARM").

## Bottom line for the original goal

"Is Kindi faster than anything existing on ARM?" is **unanswered, not answered-negative**:
the current ARM build is a scalar correctness oracle (~1 tok/s, predicted by instruction
math), while llama.cpp's ARM builds are years of NEON/dotprod optimization. The fair
comparison awaits a vectorized rows8. Meanwhile, the published phone tables must carry:
steady-state Kindi parity S24≈S23 (~0.93), cold-vs-warm caveat on all verify numbers, the
corrected chipset identities, and the 24.4/9.0 llama.cpp numbers marked
**conditions-unverified, re-run pending**.

## L2. ROOT CAUSE FOUND — Android 17 confines the adb shell to the MID cluster

On S24, **the adb shell itself** has `Cpus_allowed_list: 4-6` (taskset mask `0x70` = the three
A720@2.59 GHz cores). Verified: fresh `/proc/self/status` = 4-6; server's parent = 4-6;
**unchanged after screen wake**. Every process launched via adb shell inherits it — including
`llama-server-s24` AND the `run-as com.termux` Kindi test runs. On S23 (Android 16) the shell
gets 0-7 (all cores). **This is an OS-level cross-device confound: S24 benchmarks never saw the
X4 prime core; S23 benchmarks could.**

Second layer: on S23, the **Termux app process** (background, screen dozing) has
`Cpus_allowed_list: 0-2` = the A510 LITTLE cores. App-context cpuset is screen-state dependent.
Kindi runs via `run-as com.termux` land in app context → **screen state silently selects
little/mid/big cores for the Kindi engine**. Never controlled, never recorded.

**Fix verified live:** `taskset -a -p 3ff <pid>` widens the S24 server to all 10 cores
(mask 70→3ff, Cpus_allowed 0-9). The restriction is inherited affinity, not a hard cpuset lock.
(S24 restored to original mask 70 + dozing before owner took it.)
